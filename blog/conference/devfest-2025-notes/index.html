DevFest 2025 Notes: Developer Evolution in the Era of AI Agents

Created on December 01, 2025

2025   ·   DevFest 2025   AI Agents   Gemini   Google ADK   MCP   Software Engineering   Developer Tools   ·   Conference

Preface

I recently attended DevFest 2025, and this year’s theme undoubtedly revolved around AI Agents and Agentic Workflow. From simple Prompt Engineering to giving AI “hands and feet” to execute tasks, the entire paradigm of software development is undergoing a massive shift.

The agenda was incredibly rich, ranging from the practical experiences of independent developers to deep dives into Google’s official toolchain (Gemini CLI, ADK), and onto enterprise-level application architectures. I’ve compiled key notes from each session to record the essence of this feast of knowledge, hoping to offer a glimpse into the era of AI Agents for those who couldn’t make it.

1. The Journey of Building a Ticketing System with AI (ezBundle)

Speaker: Amos (Gao Jianlong)

This session left a deep impression on me. Amos shared how he started from a “geek’s self-perceived romance” to using AI to help himself build a fully functional ticketing and sales system, ezBundle.

The key takeaway is that while AI is powerful, it is ultimately an extension of the engineer’s will; fundamental skills are still king.

Why Build Your Own Ticketing System?

Although there are excellent ticketing systems like K-brand or A-brand on the market, the speaker encountered some unsolvable pain points:

  • Lack of Customization Flexibility: It is difficult to customize details on existing platforms.
  • Cash Flow Needs: Organizing events often requires paying high venue fees upfront (e.g., the venue fee for 2025 WebConf was as high as one million NTD). Existing platforms usually only release funds after the event ends, creating immense cash flow pressure for organizers.
  • “Solving your own problem might solve others’ too”: The goal of ezBundle was not to replace major systems, but to solve the actual problems encountered when organizing his own events.

The Real Productivity of AI Collaboration

The ezBundle system proves that AI-written code is ready for production environments:

  • Amazing Results from a One-Man Army: This project was completed by 1 engineer (no PM, no designer) using after-work hours and weekends.
  • Maximizing Efficiency: The entire project code base is about 290,000 lines. The speaker estimates he personally wrote less than 1% of the code.
  • Operational Track Record: Since its launch on 2025/9/1, it has over 1,000 members and a transaction volume nearing NT$ 2.9 million.
  • Extensive Functionality: The system not only sells tickets but also physical goods and digital content, integrating multiple payment gateways (NewebPay, ECPay, LinePay), QR Code ticket verification, questionnaires, and check-in functions.

How to Make AI “Obey”: Development Workflow in Action

The biggest challenge in using AI-assisted development is quality control to avoid generating “Tech Garbage”.

(A) Leveraging CLI and Pipes

  • AI Should Not Be Held Hostage by Editors: CLI (Command-Line Interface) tools allow AI to operate on servers and offer great flexibility.
  • The Pipe Concept: Chaining different AI models together so the output of the previous model becomes the input for the next. For example: first ask Gemini to identify potential issues, then ask Claude to provide repair suggestions and modify the code.

(B) Mastering Specifications and Design

  • Avoid Vibe Coding: Making a wish is not the same as building something usable. To get stable and expected results from AI, clear bases must be provided.
  • Few-Shot/One-Shot Examples: To ensure consistent visual style, use One-Shot or Few-Shot methods to give AI reference examples (e.g., ViewComponent previews) to “copy,” ensuring the design follows Design Guidelines.
  • TDD (Test-Driven Development): This is key to ensuring core functions (like checkout) are error-free. Even code written by AI must pass test verification.

(C) Maintaining Strict Control

  • AI is a Collaborator, Not a Decision Maker: Engineers still need to Code Review the output from AI.
  • Control Git Permissions: Never let AI operate Git on your behalf. While you can use git auto-commit to help AI generate Commit Messages, the ultimate authority (including permission to install packages) must remain in your hands.
  • Error Monitoring: Use tools like Sentry for monitoring to send immediate notifications when errors occur. This is much timelier than discovering issues through user reports.
NOTE

Conclusion: AI is a Capability Amplifier

Engineers will not be replaced by AI, but their roles will change.

  • AI is an Extension of the Driver’s Will: As long as the engineer’s own technical skills are strong enough, AI can amplify your capabilities many times over.
  • Hone Your Fundamentals: AI will amplify your good habits, but also your bad ones. In the AI era, basic skills become even more important; you must “be fiercer than the car” (be in control).
  • Role Transformation: Engineers will shift from “Code Producers” to “Code Reviewers” and “Architects + Product Thinkers”.

2. Enterprise-Grade Agentic Development in Action (Gemini-CLI + Gen AI SDK)

Speaker: Jimmy Liao (AI GDE, CTO & Co-Founder)

The core of this session was how to use Google’s Agentic technology stack to solve pain points encountered by enterprises adopting AI, providing a practical path from rapid validation to formal deployment.

Three Major Pain Points of Enterprise AI Adoption 💥

  1. Data Security Concerns: Sensitive data cannot be uploaded to external APIs; on-premise deployment or solutions with high data control are required.
  2. Tool Integration Difficulties: Internal systems (like Jira, GitLab, internal APIs) operate in silos, lacking a unified integration standard.
  3. Poor Developer Experience (DevX): The existence of multiple AI interfaces (CLI, Web UI, API) creates development chaos.

Core Tech Stack

To address these pain points, the solution is based on three core components:

Component Name Positioning/Function Note
Gemini CLI Rapid Validation & Iteration Google’s official command-line tool supporting the latest Gemini models, providing a free tier (50 req/min), suitable for rapid trial and error in the first stage.
MCP Protocol Standardized Tool Integration (Model Context Protocol) Similar to a “USB protocol” between LLMs and external tools; any tool only needs to be implemented once to be called by multiple Clients.
Google ADK Agent Automation Orchestration (Agent Development Kit) Designed for Agent-first architecture, natively supports MCP, excelling in automated orchestration of multi-step tools and context memory.

Live Demo & Benefit Highlights ✨

  • FastMCP Taiwan Stock Lookup: Used FastMCP (only 50 lines of code) to quickly wrap the Stock Exchange API into an MCP Server. Reduced manual query time from 5-10 minutes to under 5 seconds.
  • Enterprise Document Compliance Check: Implemented a Document MCP to automatically scan documents for sensitive data (PII), shortening 3-5 days of manual audit to 5 seconds.
  • Google ADK Investment Analysis: Combining multiple MCP Tools, performed automatic reasoning and complete investment analysis via an ADK Agent, with amazing efficiency gains.
TIP

Practical Analogy

The entire enterprise Agentic development process is like building a super toolbox within a company:

  • MCP is like the unified power socket specification (USB protocol), ensuring all your company’s tools (GitLab, Jira) can interface with AI models in a standardized way.
  • Google ADK plays the role of a senior technician; it can automatically determine which tools to pick up based on your voice commands and complete complex tasks precisely in order.

3. Deep Dive into ADK-A2A-MCP

Speaker: Simon Liu

This session delved into the architectural design of the AG (Agent) era. The core composition of an Agent includes: Runtime (R), Model (Brain), and Tools (Hands and Feet).

ADK: Google’s Agent Development Framework

ADK (Agent Development Kit) is an open-source framework launched by Google, supporting both Single Agent and Multi-Agent (M-Agent) architectures.

Types of Tools: The Agent decides which tool to call based on the user request (Prompt) and its own instructions:

  1. Function Tool: User-defined functions.
  2. Building Tool: ADK built-in tools (e.g., Google Search).
  3. Third-Party Tool / MCP Tool: Integrated external services, such as GitHub API.

🧠 Agent Architecture Patterns

1. Single Agent One Agent is responsible for judging and invoking the multiple tools it possesses. Pros: simple structure, low latency; Cons: difficult to handle complex Workflows.

2. Multi-Agent Distributes tasks to multiple Sub-Agents via a Coordinator/Root Agent.

  • Dispatcher / Coordinator: Root Agent decides the flow, high modularity.
  • Sequential: Pre-defined fixed order (A -> B). Predictable results, but latency stacks up.
  • Parallel: Multiple Agents execute simultaneously. High efficiency, but increased complexity and harder debugging.

A2A (Agent-to-Agent) Protocol This is the “business card” between Agents, allowing one Agent to discover and understand the capabilities and communication methods of other Agents.

WARNING

Challenges and Recommendations

The main challenges of Agent systems lie in Predictability and Stability. It is recommended to start with the simplest architecture (Single Agent) and not over-engineer complex M-Agent Patterns from the start.

4. Building No-Code AI Agents with Gemini CLI

Speaker: Will (Baoge)

Who says playing with AI Agents requires writing SDKs and connecting APIs? This session demonstrated how to use Gemini CLI to transform complex AI applications into business design tasks.

Core Positioning of Gemini CLI

Gemini CLI allows users to interact directly with models via the terminal without writing code.

  • Simplified Development: Drastically lowers the technical barrier, focusing on Agent design.
  • Rapid Prototyping: Build interactive prototypes in minutes.

Agent Practice Principles: Tools, Personas, Testing

Three key elements of building an AI Agent:

  1. Registering External Tools To give an Agent “superpowers,” the core lies in Tool Calling. Register local scripts (like Shell Scripts) as tools callable by Gemini CLI. Principle: Follow the Single Responsibility Principle, outsourcing complex execution details to independent CLI tools, letting the LLM focus on logic control.

  2. Role Setting and Prompt Optimization High-quality Agents rely on precise System Prompts:
    • Persona Definition
    • Task Instructions
    • Behavior Boundaries (Constraints)
  3. Testing and Iteration Do not try to accomplish everything with one super-long Prompt. Break it down, splitting complex processes into multiple single-function nodes.

5. New Model of Collaboration: Google ADK with Gemini CLI

Speaker: Simon Liu

This session showcased the collaborative working model of Humans and Agent Teams. The architectural design follows the principle of “Large models do big things, small models do small things.”

Architecture Planning

  • Gemini CLI / Main Agent: The primary coordinator, receiving user commands and performing macro planning.
  • Google ADK Agents / Sub Agents: Specialized sub-agents executing specific tasks.
  • Gemini CLI Extension (adk-agent-extension): The bridge connecting the two. Users issue commands via CLI, and the Main Agent calls remote ADK Agents via the Extension.

Real-World Use Cases

  • HR Agent: Assists HR in organizing the employee Onboarding process, with multiple sub-agents like Hiring, Salary, and Performance working together.
  • Database Agent: Accurately retrieves database (DuckDB/PostgreSQL) data. Through natural language queries on data trends, the Agent automatically executes SQL and returns results.
  • PM Agent: Writes PRDs and collaborates with a drawing Agent (Nano extension) to generate architecture diagrams.

Speaker: Tamas

This Workshop was very interesting. To energize everyone at the start, Tamas asked everyone to stand up and exercise. He told everyone to raise their hands and clap fast. Just as everyone was following along seriously, he suddenly took out his phone, turned around, and took a selfie with the crowd. This sudden move was really funny and instantly warmed up the atmosphere.

After the relaxed opening, Tamas guided everyone step-by-step, from basics to practice, teaching how to build a standard MCP Server.

(The content was mainly a hands-on session, focusing on experiencing how to transform existing APIs into MCP Servers, enabling AI to understand and use these APIs.)

Summary

After attending DevFest 2025, my deepest feeling is: The role of the engineer is evolving rapidly.

We are no longer just “Coders”; we are transforming into “AI Coordinators” and “Architects.” Tools like Gemini CLI, Google ADK, and the MCP protocol are paving the way for this new era.

The future development model will be Humans defining goals, AI Agent teams collaborating to execute. Our core value will depend on how we design these Agents, how we define their communication methods, and how we ensure the quality and safety of their output.

This is an exciting era; let’s embrace the change and dance with AI!
